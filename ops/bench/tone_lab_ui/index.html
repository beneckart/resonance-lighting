– i Proximity ding turns each lantern's distance sensor into a trigger: wave a hand in front of it and it chimes once at its own pulse. When switched on, each sensor learns its background for 1.5 s (keep hands away); after that only something at least 30 cm nearer than the background and inside the range counts. The laptop watches every sensor about 8 times a second over USB and sends the strike, so this works only while the lanterns are plugged in and awake. A card saying "sensor blocked" has something right in front of it.
i Waking: a sleeping lantern is off USB and radio, so it cannot be woken instantly. It wakes by itself about every 2 minutes; Wake and Wake all tell the app to catch it then and open a 60-minute session, so it can take up to ~2 minutes. Keep awake also tops up every awake lantern's session before it runs out, for as long as this app is running and the lanterns are plugged in. Pulse saves as you type (blank = firmware default 40 ms)

Timing i Sync latency strikes each lantern in the current set a few times and listens with the laptop microphone. It measures how long each one takes from command to sound and saves that on its card (median, plus the spread between strikes). Keep the room quiet; about 2 seconds per strike. Re-sync a lantern after changing its pulse, since pulse width changes how fast the mallet arrives. i Compensate latency changes when commands are sent. Each lantern has its own delay from command to chime. With this on, faster lanterns get their command sent early by however much quicker they are than the slowest one, so every chime lands exactly on the beat. Example: latencies of 30, 55 and 40 ms are sent 25, 0 and 15 ms early. Uses the latencies from Sync latency; unsynced lanterns get the group median. Applies to Play and to Patterns. i Measure timing on play only listens; it never changes what is sent. The microphone records the run, finds where each chime starts, and compares the hits with a perfect grid at your gap. The log shows each lantern's error (+ late, - early), the worst hit (max) and the typical error (rms). Compare runs with compensation off and on. Needs a quiet room and a gap of at least 100 ms.

    i Patterns are a step sequencer: rows are lanterns (tone order, highest at top), columns are steps. Click or drag across cells to place hits; edits save automatically. Each lantern plays at its own pulse. Hits on one lantern must be at least 250 ms apart, counting the loop wrap, so its chime capacitor can refill; red cells break that rule and the pattern will not play. This plays over USB from the laptop. Playing without the laptop needs the firmware pattern mode in the proposed ADR 0084.
    Standalone i Standalone playback runs inside the lanterns (ADR 0084), so it keeps going after you unplug them. Load onto lanterns stores each lantern's part (its hits, pulse and a mechanical lead) in its memory and checks it with a checksum; lanterns with no hits still join as silent timekeepers. Play standalone starts every connected lantern for the chosen minutes: they elect a conductor among themselves, which broadcasts the beat over radio so all of them stay in time. Playback ends after the time runs out, on Stop, on low battery, or if a lantern resets. Needs the pattern-mode firmware; older lanterns are reported as not supported.
    Mode i Each chart is one lantern's distance sensor over the last 15 seconds, drawn the way it looks: the sensor is at the top, looking down. Dots are returns (faint = weak, solid = confident enough to count as a hand, green = counted as a hand). The gray line is the learned background, the amber dashed line is how close a hand must come (30 cm nearer than the background), and the green dashed line is your range. Red marks are dings; purple dashed marks are dings that Watch only would have played. Keep hands away for 1.5 s after switching on or relearning. Sunlight weakens these infrared sensors.

    Sensors look straight down from the bottom of each lantern and ignore anything closer than 8 cm: wave a hand 10-50 cm below a lantern, not beside it.
